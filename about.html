About this map.

A small website with one job: help a new student or a visitor find the right building without asking around.

Why I built it

When I arrived on campus, the map I had was a flat plan: useful in a meeting room, awkward on a phone. The buildings all looked similar, and the names were easy to mix up. I wanted something I could open in a browser, tap, and understand in a few seconds — so I drew one myself.

The result is a schematic map, not a satellite photo. Shapes are simplified and labelled, which makes the important things — where the teaching buildings sit, how the student dorms line the eastern edge, how the restaurant and teacher dormitories sit around the lake — easier to read at any size.

What is on the site

  • Home — a short introduction and a preview of the campus.
  • Map — the main page: 14 buildings and 11 places inside them, a search box, category filters, and a details panel with a photo of whatever you click.
  • Buildings — the same places as cards, each with its photograph and the rooms inside it.
  • About — this page.

How it works

Everything is static. There is no server and no database, and the site does not collect any data.

  • Semantic HTML — one <header>, one <main>, one <footer>; the map is an inline SVG with a title and description.
  • One stylesheet — colours, spacing and category colours live in CSS custom properties, so the four pages look like one site.
  • Vanilla JavaScript — a single small file reads the building data, handles clicks and keyboard input, and filters the map. No libraries.
  • Responsive by default — the SVG scales with its container; on a narrow screen the map becomes scrollable instead of shrinking to nothing.
  • Accessible controls — every building is a keyboard-focusable button, the markers inside buildings are buttons of their own, the filter buttons report their pressed state, and the details panel announces changes.
  • Photos on demand — the panel builds its <img> only when you click something, so opening the map downloads no photographs at all.
  • English / 中文 — the switch in the header changes the language of the whole site, and remembers your choice.

What I learned

  • How to plan an SVG with a coordinate system, and why a viewBox makes a drawing responsive for free.
  • How to keep a map interaction simple: select, filter, describe — no framework needed.
  • What a photograph weighs. Full-size pictures are 2–5 MB each, so a page that loads one on demand feels very different from one that loads twenty.
  • How much easier a project becomes when the scope is small on purpose. Four pages, one clear job.

What I would add next

  • More rooms inside the teaching buildings.
  • A simple route hint: “You are at the Welcome Center; the Student Center is this way.”
  • More places — the library, the main gate — once their names are confirmed.
